Using a 114-point dataset assembled from four inde-
pendent facilities [8, 19, 13, 20], the second companion manuscript [5] finds that a four-predictor
power law with a high in-sample fit collapses under leave-one-facility-out cross-validation
(pooled LOFO R2 of−0.885), and only the Reynolds-number exponent survives bootstrap
resampling acrossfacilities. A subsequent sweep of twenty-one further attempts, sixteen ofthem
learningmethods—LLM-guidedphysical-lawdiscovery,ahierarchicalBayesianneuralnetwork
with a physics-informed prior, Gaussian processes and a deep-kernel variant, a set transformer,
domain-adversarial networks, model-agnostic meta-learning, genetic symbolic regression and a
physics-informed network, among others — was run on the same 114 points. Every one returns
a negative pooled LOFO R2, and ten of them scoreworse than the four-predictor linear fit
they were meant to improve on. That study also quantifies why: the largest canonical correla-
tion between its predictor block and the facility indicators is 0.996, so one predictor direction
is very nearly a relabelling of facility identity, while the residual Reynolds-weighted direction
is almost unconfounded (0.005).
That negative result is informative, but it leaves a methodological question open, and
one real dataset cannot close it: with four facilities, the smallest holding eight points, is the
LOFO+bootstrapprotocolitselftrustworthyatthatscale?Anegativeverdictmeanssomething
only if the protocol reliably separates genuine non-generalization from a false alarm driven by
thin data, one where the law is in fact universal and the protocol says otherwise. Settling that
needs known ground truth, and only synthetic data supplies it.
This paper studies the audit protocol itself. The real windage dataset it leaves alone, the
second companion manuscript having already treated it exhaustively. Under what combinations
of domain countK, points per domainn, noise level, and type and strength of confounding
does a LOFO+bootstrap protocol correctly tell a universal law from a facility-specific artifact?
We generate thousands of synthetic multi-facility datasets whose ground truth is declared, not
inferred. On each we run the same class of audit protocol the companion manuscripts use,
implementing and reporting both LOFO and facility-level bootstrap. We then train a meta-
model over the resulting scenarios to predict protocol reliability, using statistics a practitioner
could compute from their own data together with the protocol’s own observed verdict, never
the generator parameters that fixed the ground truth. We then evaluate that meta-model using
the real windage case’s ownobserved LOFO verdict as an external check, citing only figures
already reported in the companion manuscript, without re-analyzing its raw data.
Contributions.
1. A synthetic benchmark (4,000 scenarios under Gaussian parameter drift, plus a second
pass of 8,000 scenarios spanning four confounding mechanisms crossed with equal/unequal
facility sizes) for evaluating cross-domain scaling-law audit protocols, with both LOFO
cross-validation and facility-level bootstrap implemented, matching the companion studies’
own methodology.
2. A quantified characterization of when the conventional LOFO> 0 threshold rule succeeds
and fails: robust to parameter drift and heavy noise, but with a severe blind spot against
nonlinear misspecification and against clustered/partial universality that, for the nonlinear
mechanism, does not shrink with more data or more facilities.
3. A facility-label permutation test computed from the same LOFO fits, shown to be a
substantially more reliable declaration rule than the plain threshold across all four
confounding mechanisms, at no additional data-collection cost.
4. A third pass (6,000 scenarios) that adds covariate-support overlap between facilities as a
design dimension, showing that the permutation test’s validity is conditional on it: the
naive test is anti-conservative once operating windows separate, a stratified variant
restores calibration without losing power, and near-total separation is an identifiability
limit for any label-permutation scheme. This yields a decision rule keyed to an observable
statistic, which we also evaluate on the companion corpus.

3.3. Meta-model
For each pass we train a gradient-boosting classifier to predict LOFO-verdict correctness. Its
inputs are quantities a practitioner running this protocol could actually compute on their
own data: the facility countK, the mean facility size n, the protocol’s own observed ver-
dict (protocol_says_universal), and two proxies taken from the pooled and per-facility
fits. Those are obs_sigma_hat, the residual standard deviation of the single pooled fit, and
obs_hetero_hat, the standard deviation across facilities of each facility’sown individually-fit
Reynolds-like coefficient. Neither proxy requires knowing whether the underlying law is truly
universal. Two exclusions need stating outright, since external review caught both (see Limita-
tions). The first is the ground-truth label, unobservable in practice and tautologically tied to
the correctness target. The second is the generator’s latentsigma and heterogeneity settings,
which an earlier draft used directly even though they are generator parameters and not quan-
tities anyone could compute from real data. For the second pass we train two separate models
on the same observable feature set: anobservable-only model matching the first pass exactly,
and a secondoracle model additionally given the confounding mechanism as an input. Con-
found type is not observable in practice. An earlier draft folded it into a single pooled accuracy
figure and called the whole result observable-only, which was imprecise; that is corrected here
(see Limitations). We report the oracle model separately, as a what-if upper bound and not as
a claim about achievable performance. A Gaussian process classifier is trained on a subsample
of the first pass as a probabilistic cross-check. Permutation importance quantifies which inputs
drive predicted reliability in the observable-only models.

4.1. Overall protocol reliability (first pass, Gaussian drift)
The first pass runs 4,000 Gaussian-drift scenarios, 2,013 of them truly universal and 1,987
truly confounded. The LOFO> 0 threshold verdict is correct 90.1% of the time overall. Split
by ground truth, that is 100.0% when the law is universal and 80.0% when it is not. The
facility-level bootstrap’s Reynolds-exponent confidence interval excludes zero (the coefficient
“survives”) 100.0% of the time when truly universal and 71.8% of the time when truly con-
founded under this mechanism. That is close to the threshold verdict itself here, if slightly less
reliable, which fits bootstrap and LOFO testing related but not identical things: coefficient
stability against held-out predictive fit.
The permutation-test rule is more reliable at no extra data-collection cost.
The facility-label permutation test (Method) computed from the same pooled fits raises
overall accuracy to 96.5%: 94.2% when truly universal (a small increase in false alarms rel-
ative to the threshold rule’s 100.0%) and 98.7% when truly confounded (a large gain over
the threshold rule’s 80.0%). The trade is real: a handful of false “confounded” calls on gen-
uinely universal scenarios, in exchange for a much larger reduction in missed detections. At
theα = 0.05 significance level used throughout, the net effect strongly favors the permutation
rule, and Section 4.2 shows the gain is largest precisely where the threshold rule is weakest.

The distinction matters, because LOFO predicts one held-out facility at a time. A stratified
permutation has admissible label swaps for a fold only when the held-out facility shares covari-
ate range with the rest, so one separated facility disables the test for its own fold, however well
the remaining facilities agree among themselves. A corpus can thus post a comfortable mean
and a marginal minimum. The companion corpus below is that case. Both statistics come from
the design matrix alone; we key on the minimum because it binds.
The naive permutation test is calibrated only where the folds overlap well. Its false-alarm
rate climbs as the operating windows separate: 5.6–6.8% in the top two bins, which is about the
nominalα = 0.05 and the regime both earlier passes occupy, against 17.5% in the lowest. That
is roughlythreefold anti-conservative. The mechanism is the one the Method anticipates. With
separated windows, predicting a held-out facility means extrapolating, while the permuted
pseudo-folds keep interpolating, so a universal law looks anomalously bad against its own null.
By construction the threshold rule cannot do this, and it produced no false alarm at any overlap
level in this pass, which is what makes it a safe fallback.
Stratifying the permutation repairs this cheaply. Confining swaps to within a stratum holds
the false-alarm rate at 4.4–6.8% across the whole range, and every bin’s 95% interval in Table 5
covers the nominal 0.05. The power cost is small: 97.6% in the lowest bin, 100% elsewhere,
against 81.8–88.5% for the threshold rule. On both criteria at once, then, the stratified test is
the best of the three, and we recommend it wherever it is admissible.
Where the advantage ends.
The lowest bin of Table 5 still shows the stratified test working, so the bound-
ary lies inside it. Two further batches locate it ( results/overlap_fine_sweep.json,
results/overlap_transition.json). The transition is abrupt, and it sits at zero. Where
every fold retains some positive overlap the stratified test keeps full power: 98.4% at min-
imum fold overlap 0.003–0.006, 98.5% at 0.006–0.010, 100.0% at 0.010–0.020. Once one
14 fold is exactly disjoint, power falls to 56.2% ( n=162; lowest_bin_split_at_exact_zero
in results/overlap_fine_sweep.json). That fold admits no label swap, so it contributes
nothing and the remaining folds carry the test alone. When all facilities are mutu-
ally disjoint the test goes inert. A dedicated batch of 150 scenarios per arm at overlap
= 0 (degenerate_corner_overlap_zero in results/synth_scenarios_v3_summary.json)
returns 0.0% false alarms and 0.0% power. Facility identity has become a deterministic func-
tion of the covariates, every stratum holds a single facility, and nothing can be rejected. The
naive test in that same corner reaches 20.0% false alarms; the threshold rule holds 0.0% at
85.3% power. This inertia is a property of the problem, not a defect of the estimator. Under
perfect separation, “the facilities follow different laws” and “the facilities were run at different
conditions” cannot be told apart by any argument that permutes facility labels.

5. Discussion
The recommendation this study supports is a two-step procedure rather than a single rule.
Figure 8 states it in full.First, measure how far your data sources actually overlap in covariate
space. The statistic is the minimum over leave-one-out folds of the intersection-over-union
between the held-out source’s range and the union of the others’ (Method). It needs nothing
but the design matrix. Take the minimum, not an average across pairs: one separated source
disables the test for its own fold however well the others agree.Second, choose the declaration
rule accordingly:
• Every fold shares at least one observation with the rest: use thestratified
facility-label permutation test. The criterion is a point count, not a range comparison, and
the threshold is one (Table 6); where the count is one or two, treat a rejection as
suggestive, since calibration there is only approximately nominal. It is computed from the
same fits a LOFO audit already produces, requires no additional data collection, closes
most of the gap between the threshold rule’s best mechanism (Gaussian drift, 82.3%
detection) and its worst (nonlinear misspecification, 9.4% detection), and unlike the naive
permutation test it stays calibrated as the sources separate (Table 5).
• Some fold shares no observation at all with the rest: use the plain LOFO> 0
threshold. It detects less, but it is by far the most conservative of the three: no false alarm
18 at any overlap level in the third pass, and 4 in 3,959 universal scenarios across the second. A
stratified test drops to 60.7% power there, below the threshold rule’s own 80.0% (Table 6).
Figure 8. The decision rule. The quantity that governs the choice is a count on the worst leave-
one-facility-out fold, computable from the design matrix before any test is run.
The naive permutation test is the one we would now advise against as a default, though
the first two passes alone would have recommended it without qualification. It is the strongest
option when supports overlap and the weakest when they do not, and the LOFO output alone
does not tell a practitioner which case they are in. That the earlier passes could not see this
carries a lesson of its own. A benchmark that samples one corner of the design space will
confidently recommend a method that fails outside it.
Where a LOFO+bootstrap audit has already been run with the plain threshold, as in the
companion manuscripts, this study’s role is instead diagnostic: knowing which regime the
threshold rule is in changes how much its verdict should be trusted.
A second implication cuts against the intuitive fix, which is to collect more facilities. Within
the range tested, once the type and strength of confounding are accounted for, raw facility
count predicts the threshold rule’s reliability poorly. For nonlinear misspecification it does
worse than that: more facilities make the ruleworse (Section 4.2). The practical guidance this
study supports is:know, or test for, the shape of the suspected confounding before trusting a
threshold-rule LOFO verdict. Or use a permutation rule, which does not require knowing it,
once you have checked that the sources overlap enough for one to be valid.A threshold-rule
audit that declares “universal” should not be read as ruling out facility-specific structure if that
structure could plausibly be nonlinear or clustered. The threshold rule is nearly blind to both;
the permutation rules are not (Table 3). What does help is arranging that the sources share
operating conditions, which can be designed for in advance. Overlap is what buys access to the
more powerful rules (Table 5). It is a property of how a corpus is assembled, not of how many
facilities it holds. One implication follows for the second companion manuscript [5], which used
the threshold rule. Its finding ofnon-generalization sits in the regime where that rule is reliable,
and Section 4.6 shows the generator design here rarely returns a false “not universal” call on
truly universal data. Confirming it more specifically is possible in principle, since the corpus
does meet the admissibility criterion (Section 4.5). It meets it by one observation, though,
in the regime where calibration is least certain. Any such reanalysis would carry that caveat,
and could not stand as a clean second opinion. Any future work in this series reporting a
positive generalization result from the threshold rule alone should be treated cautiously unless
nonlinear and clustered alternatives have been separately ruled out, ideally by also checking
the permutation-rule verdict.
6. Limitations
The generator uses one base functional form, the two-predictor log-linear power law that won
in the second companion manuscript [5]. Other functional forms, higher-dimensional predictor
sets and non-log-linear ground truths go unexplored. Each scenario is a single random draw,
never repeated across seeds, so scenario-level accuracy estimates carry sampling noise the point
estimates do not fully show (binomial 95% confidence intervals are reported in Table 3 for the
headline per-mechanism figures; individual cells in finer breakdowns, e.g.K bins in Figure 4,
are noisier still and not individually interval-estimated). The real-case anchor sweeps both
observable proxies over a plausible range instead of computing their true values, by design, to
avoid re-analyzing the companion manuscript’s raw data. So it is illustrative sensitivity analysis
and not a precise recovery. Its two meta-models also disagree by a wide margin (Section 4.6).
Both facts should temper the weight put on it.
Three quantities are excluded from the meta-model’s feature set, and a reader should know
each was once included by mistake. The scenario’s ground-truth label, which trivially deter-
mines the correctness target. The generator’s latent noise and heterogeneity settings, not
computable from real data despite an earlier claim otherwise. And the confounding mecha-
nism, kept in the oracle variant alone and reported apart from the observable-only result. They
are recorded because they bear on how the accuracy numbers should be read, not as narrative
detail.
A bug in the clustered-mechanism generator, fixed before the results reported here, could
assign every facility to the same latent cluster with probability≈ 1/2K−1 per scenario. That
silently mislabeled a handful of nominally confounded clustered scenarios as truly universal,
about 5% of draws at smallK. Every second-pass number in this manuscript postdates the
fix, which is rejection sampling requiring at least two distinct clusters. We flag it so that a
reader comparing against an earlier internal draft does not meet an unexplained shift in the
clustered-mechanism figures.

The fallback is the plain threshold rule,
much the most conservative of the three, with 4 false alarms across 3,959 universal second-pass
scenarios and none at all in the overlap sweep. It takes over under near-total separation, where
no label-permutation argument can separate a different law from a different design. Which
rule applies depends on statistics a practitioner can compute from their own design matrix:
the minimum across leave-one-out folds, not an average across pairs, and a point count in
place of a range comparison. We define them and calibrate the criterion. At least one shared
observation on the worst fold licenses the stratified test, with calibration that is only approx-
imately nominal at that margin; none at all costs it 39 points of power. An observable-only
meta-model estimator, applied to the real windage case’s own observed threshold-rule verdict
[6, 5], returns near-certainty. That certainty is largely tautological. Its two implementations
span 0.627–0.999, and we verify directly that the higher number reflects a structural property
of the generator, not case-specific evidence. What survives is a narrower claim: a false-positive
confounding call is an unlikely explanation for that verdict. It is an illustrative check, and not
independent confirmation that the real case’s mechanism is one this protocol reliably detects.
The three manuscripts together cover the audit protocol, its application to a real corpus, and
the boundaries of the protocol’s own reliability, including a remedy for its main weakness and
the conditions under which that remedy holds.
A. Minimum Verifiable Facts from the Companion Manuscripts
The motivation for this paper, and the external check in Section 4.6, rest on numbers from two
unpublished companion manuscripts [6, 5]. A reader of this paper alone cannot inspect them.
To limit how much of the argument sits on our own say-so, we restate the small set of facts
those sections actually use. Each was checked against the companion manuscripts themselves,
not reproduced from memory:
• The real windage dataset hasN = 114 points across four facilities, with reported sizes 45,
41, 20, and 8.
• The companion study’s winning pooled model (log-linear, four Buckingham-Π predictors)
reaches in-sample R2= 0.870 but pooled leave-one-facility-out R2=−0.885 in log space.
• Of that model’s coefficients, only the Reynolds-number exponent’s bootstrap confidence
interval excludes zero; the companion manuscript reports this as the sole coefficient that
survives facility-level bootstrap resampling.
• The four facilities’log10Re windows are [6.80, 7.91] (Guo, n=45), [5.47, 6.82] (Liu, n=20),
[4.96, 6.72] (Vrancik,n=41) and [5.57, 6.51] (Zheng, n=8), giving pairwise support overlaps
of 0.009, 0.000 and 0.000 between the largest facility and each of the other three, and
0.530–0.691 among those three: a mean pairwise overlap of 0.317, but a minimum fold
overlap of 0.008 (Section 4.5).
• The number of each facility’s points lying inside thelogRe range spanned by the other
three, per leave-one-facility-out fold, is 1 of 45 (Guo), 20 of 20 (Liu), 31 of 41 (Vrancik) and
8 of 8 (Zheng). The corpus spansReΩ from 9.06× 104 to 8.15× 107, a 900-fold range.
• The companion study’s own identifiability index reports a largest canonical correlation of
0.996 between its four-predictor block and the facility indicators, with a residual
Reynolds-weighted direction at 0.005.
Eight window endpoints and four counts: that is the whole of what Section 4.5’s conclusion
about this corpus rests on. Both overlap statistics follow from them without the dataset.
The rounded windows give a mean pairwise overlap of 0.318 and a minimum fold overlap of
0.007, against 0.317 and 0.008 from the full data, the gap being the rounding printed here.
from_published_windows in code/companion_overlap.py performs the recomputation.
The last two items say where each facility sits in Reynolds space and how many of its points
share range with the others. Both describe the corpus’s design, not the windage relationship,
so reporting them does not breach the scope limit below; the first is in principle readable from
the four original published sources [8, 19, 13, 20]. They appear because Section 4.5 makes
the choice of declaration rule depend on them, and no reader can check that choice without
them. code/companion_overlap.py computes them from the compiled dataset file held by
the companion study’s authors. We do not redistribute that file, only the aggregate statistics
it yields (results/companion_overlap.json). The script exits without effect when the file is
absent, so every other result in this repository reproduces without it.
We do not restate, and did not re-derive, any finer-grained breakdown (e.g. per-facility fitted
coefficients or Cp values) from the companion manuscripts’ raw data, since doing so would
constitute the re-analysis this paper’s scope deliberately excludes (Introduction). A reader who
needs to audit these facts directly should request the companion manuscripts or their eventual
published versions from the corresponding author.
